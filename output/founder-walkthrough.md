# Founder Walkthrough — Last Agency

**Persona:** Indu — second-time founder, D2C wellness brand, just closed a Series A. Two months in. CAC has crept from ₹680 to ₹980 in six weeks. Board's "marketing hire" track is on the next agenda. Heard about Last Agency from a peer founder over WhatsApp.
**Context:** 11:47 p.m., bed, on her phone. Husband asleep. She has maybe ninety seconds before she puts the phone down and decides in the morning.
**Outcome:** **Fills the form** — but not on the first scroll. She bounces to Verdant Ritual mid-homepage, comes back, fills the form from the case-study page closing CTA. Total time on site: 4 minutes 12 seconds. The thing that closes it is the line "I'd been told by three other agencies that my repeat-rate problem was a product problem."

---

## The first 5 seconds (homepage hero)

She lands. Phone is dim. The first thing she registers is the three small-caps lines stacked at the top:

> a client trained their in-house team using our playbook.
> then they stopped paying us.
> **we sent them a thank-you note.**

The third line is in amber. That's the moment — five seconds in — where her thumb stops scrolling and her eyes go up instead of down. She reads it twice. The setup-setup-punchline structure is doing the work of three paragraphs of positioning copy. She doesn't have language yet for "the exit is the product," but the line has installed the thesis without using the word "exit."

Then her eyes drop to the headline:

> your last agency. *we mean that literally.*

Lowercase. Italic accent on the second half. Confident without shouting. The word "literally" is doing a lot — it pre-empts the eye-roll she would have had at "your last agency" if it were a tagline alone. The sub-hero pulls the kill-shot specific: "twelve to eighteen months. Train your in-house hire. Hand over the playbook. Then we leave. The exit is the product."

The CTA reads "Hire us. Then unfollow us." On a phone, at midnight, with a sleeping husband — she smiles. That is a real human smile, not a metric. She doesn't tap it.

**What works:** the three opening lines. They're the closest thing to a perfect founder-empathetic hook on the site. The "thank-you note" line lands the entire business model in eight words.
**What's friction:** none in the first five seconds.
**Voice slips:** none.
**Close-tab risk:** zero. She scrolls.

---

## The first 30 seconds (down to philosophy anchor)

She scrolls past the marquee ("paid. lifecycle. brand. handoff. exit.") — it reads to her as a tag cloud but in the brand's voice. She doesn't stop. She hits the Services section:

> four things. in order. *then we go.*

That italicized "then we go" is the third time the exit beat has fired in under twenty seconds, and it's still feeling earned, not over-played. She skims the four pillar cards on mobile — they stack vertically. The Build → The Run → The Handoff → The Exit. Each one has a month range. **Months 0–3. Months 3–12. Months 12–15. Month 15+.** She does the math in her head: that's exactly the cadence her board would buy. Not a year-on-year retainer. A start date and an end date.

She scrolls into the philosophy anchor. It pins and floats:

> retainers are *a tax on indecision.*

Body copy underneath: *"Most agencies bill forever. We bill for fourteen months. The math works because we build the function, train your hire, hand over the playbook, and leave. The exit is the product."*

This is the moment she'd bookmark on a desktop. On mobile she screenshots it and sends it to her co-founder. The screenshot becomes the artifact that survives the night.

**What works:** the philosophy anchor lands. The "tax on indecision" line is the one she'd quote back in the form. The sticky behavior holds attention without being annoying.
**What's friction:** the hero reel video is missing in placeholder state — the `onError` hide is invisible to her, but the hero feels slightly flat without motion behind it. Minor. Not a tab-closer.
**Voice slips:** none caught. The sub-headline on Services has a "fixed term. fixed scope. no auto-renew." line that lands the legal/commercial commitment without sounding legalistic.
**Close-tab risk:** still zero.

## The services moment

Indu doesn't go to /services. She doesn't need to. The homepage's four-pillar block already answered "what do I get for my money?" She got: a 3-month build, a 9-month run, a 3-month handoff, a 1-month exit. Months are specific. Deliverables are specific (LTV model, creative brief template, weekly numbers, monthly board doc, Friday review, 40-page playbook, a Loom per chapter).

What she did NOT get from the homepage pillars: the price. That is intentional — the voice guide is clear that "fixed term. fixed scope." is the pricing posture, with the actual number reserved for the conversation. For Indu specifically, this is fine — she's pre-qualifying for fit, not for budget. She's a Series A founder; the budget will sort itself out. **But** I want to flag this: a more cost-sensitive founder might bounce here looking for a price band. The site is correctly opinionated, but it's an opinion that pays a small cost on the bottom of the funnel.

**What works:** four pillars stack cleanly on mobile. Numbered 01–04 in amber. The pillar 01 body now correctly reads "Three months. Fixed scope." matching its own subhead.
**What's friction:** no price band anywhere. Conscious choice; flagging for awareness.
**Voice slips:** none.

## The case study moment

She scrolls past the Services block into PortfolioStripA. Horizontal-scroll cards on mobile. Her thumb swipes left. The first card she actually focuses on is **Verdant Ritual** — wellness, female founder, the headline reads "Rebuilding Verdant Ritual's repeat rate from 18% to 51% and cutting paid dependency in half."

She taps the card.

The case study hero loads. Breadcrumb: work → Verdant Ritual. Industry pill: • wellness. The brand name in display lowercase: *verdant ritual.* The headline below it, the services pipe-separated ("The Build | The Run | The Handoff"), and a status counter pill: **6 months standalone.** Six months is recent enough to feel real and old enough to count.

She skims the four chapters. The Handoff chapter is the one that gets her — pull-quote treatment, left border in amber, italic:

> *"Months 12-15 we trained Tara, who'd been promoted from coordinator to Lifecycle Lead. She ran the November launch end to start, including the segmentation tree. We reviewed flows once a week, then once a month, then not at all."*

The phrase "then not at all" is the moment the entire thesis lands for her. Not "we mentored her" — *we reviewed flows once a week, then once a month, then not at all.* That's the exit shaped as a fade-out. She gets it.

Then the testimonial. Long-form. Sienna Holloway, Brooklyn. The line that closes it for Indu:

> *"I'd been told by three other agencies that my repeat-rate problem was a product problem. Last Agency looked at the same data and said it was a flow problem. They were right — we went from 18 to 51 percent without changing a single SKU."*

Three things this testimonial does that Indu specifically needs:
1. **Names other agencies as wrong.** Indu has already worked with two agencies. Both told her something her gut knew was wrong.
2. **Specific numbers without bragging.** 18 → 51%. Not "significant growth." Not "transformative." Just the number.
3. **The ending: "I send Aman a Diwali card now. That's the relationship."** This is the relationship she wants. Not a Slack channel. A Diwali card.

The closing CTA on the case study: *"same shape engagement. your name in this slot next."* She reads "your name in this slot next" and looks at the URL bar where it says `lastagency.com/work/verdant-ritual`. The slot framing lands.

**What works:** Verdant Ritual is the perfect case study for Indu. The handoff pull-quote is the strongest editorial moment on the site. The testimonial does brand and conversion work simultaneously.
**What's friction:** the hero block is a tinted-color placeholder (no real photography yet). For a wellness brand, photography matters. This is on the MANUAL-WORK list and known. **Indu's specific friction:** at midnight, on a phone, the absence of a real product shot makes Verdant feel slightly less real than it is. She didn't bounce, but a more skeptical founder might.
**Voice slips:** none. The testimonial is verbatim from Phase 2 and clean.

## The contact moment

She taps the closing CTA: "tell us what's bleeding." It routes to /get-in-touch.

The contact hero mirrors the homepage:

> hire us. *then unfollow us.*

Body: "We read every note. Expect a reply in one to two business days — usually less. If we're not the right fit, we'll say so in forty-eight hours."

That last line is the friction-killer. Most agency contact pages threaten to follow up forever. This one threatens to tell her no within 48 hours. That's a confident position and it lowers her cost-of-contact.

The form is five fields:

1. **your name** — placeholder "Priya — Head of Growth"
2. **company** — placeholder "series-A consumer brand" *(this placeholder is **perfect** for Indu specifically — she sees her own situation echoed in the example)*
3. **email**
4. **where you're at right now** — textarea, placeholder "monthly ad spend, what's working, what's broken, what your in-house plan looks like."
5. **budget posture** — dropdown: pick one / we're scaling fast / tight runway / exploring / ready to hire

She picks "ready to hire."

The submit button reads **"Send. We reply Monday."** It's 11:51 p.m. Sunday night. The word "Monday" is a real promise — one day from now. She taps it.

She lands on /thanks: *"noted. we'll be in touch."* Body: *"The reply comes from a founder. Not a chatbot. Not an intake form."*

**What works:**
- Form is short. Five fields, plus a dropdown. Submittable on mobile in under 90 seconds if she's writing fast.
- Placeholders are voiced and specific. "monthly ad spend, what's working, what's broken, what your in-house plan looks like" prompts her to write substantively, not minimally.
- "We reply Monday" makes a one-day commitment visible at the moment she taps submit.
- Error messages are quiet and voiced ("name's required.", "that doesn't look like an email.", "tell us more — at least a sentence.")
- The "we'll say so in forty-eight hours" line in the hero lowers the cost of being told no.

**What's friction:**
- **The `email's required.` error uses a curly apostrophe (’) where the other errors use a straight apostrophe (').** Tiny visual inconsistency in error states. Caught and fixed inline below.
- The dropdown's `↓` chevron is a Unicode arrow, not a styled SVG — on iOS Safari mobile, the native select can show its own caret over it. Cosmetic, not blocking.
- No client-side persistence of the form text. If Indu accidentally swipes back, her draft is gone. (This is a MANUAL-WORK item, not a brand issue.)

**Voice slips caught:** the email error apostrophe. Fixed.
**Close-tab risk at submission:** approximately zero. The 48-hour-no promise and the founder-replies-not-chatbot line carry her over the line.

## Voice slips caught

Reviewing the rendered output as Indu reads it:

1. **ContactForm email error** had a curly apostrophe (`email’s required.`) while every other error in the file used a straight apostrophe (`name's required.`, `that doesn't look like an email.`). Inconsistent punctuation in inline errors is the kind of detail a typography-literate founder notices on a phone. **Fixed inline.** File: `src/components/sections/contact/ContactForm.tsx`.

2. **ClosingCTA body** uses the curly `’` in `Aman or Priya replies`. Reviewed — this one is intentional Next/React rendering through `&apos;`, displays as a curly typographic apostrophe site-wide. Consistent across the codebase. No change.

3. **Hero copy line 2** — *"then they stopped paying us."* Word choice is voice-perfect. No slip.

4. **Verdant Ritual testimonial** — "I send Aman a Diwali card now. That's the relationship." This is verbatim from `case-studies.json` and Brand Guardian's Phase 2 approval. It lands. No slip.

No other voice slips found in this walk.

## Moments of friction

In rank order of impact:

1. **Missing case-study photography on the Verdant hero** — for a wellness founder visiting at midnight, the absence of a real product shot is the only place the site's polish dips below the voice's confidence level. The site SAYS "we ship real things." The hero IMAGE says "placeholder." This is a known MANUAL-WORK item but is the single biggest gap between brand-promise and on-page reality.

2. **Hero reel video missing** — the homepage hero's background video element is silent (file not shipped, `onError` hides it). The GradientBlob accent carries the visual weight, which works at a static-design level, but the hero lacks the kind of motion that the rest of the site's animations promise. Again, known MANUAL-WORK.

3. **No price band anywhere** — intentional, and brand-correct. Flagged only because a fraction of founders will bounce looking for a number to compare against an in-house hire. Not a brand violation; a positioning trade-off.

4. **Filter-chip touch targets on /work** — 36 px tall on mobile, below the 44×44 WCAG ideal. Indu didn't visit /work directly (she came in via PortfolioStripA on the homepage), but a founder who lands on /work first will notice that chips feel slightly fiddly on mobile. Logged in build-report.md.

5. **The dropdown caret on the budget select** — Unicode arrow rather than a styled SVG, slightly inelegant on iOS. Cosmetic.

## Moments of brand resonance

The five moments that most reinforce the thesis, in order of impact:

1. **The three-line hero opener** — "a client trained their in-house team using our playbook. / then they stopped paying us. / we sent them a thank-you note." The setup-setup-punchline lands the entire business model in eight beats. This is the single best paragraph on the site.

2. **The Verdant Ritual handoff pull-quote** — *"We reviewed flows once a week, then once a month, then not at all."* The fade-out cadence does the editorial work of a chapter in twelve words.

3. **The Verdant testimonial close** — *"I send Aman a Diwali card now. That's the relationship."* The Indian cultural anchor + the post-engagement relationship + the founder's first name all in one sentence. This is the line that closes the funnel.

4. **"Hire us. Then unfollow us."** as a hero CTA, repeated on the contact hero — the line is in the brand's voice, it's a verb, it's specific (unfollow is a real action a founder takes on Twitter/Instagram), and it carries the exit-beat into the action button. Best CTA copy on the site.

5. **"We'll say so in forty-eight hours."** on the contact hero — the moment the site stops selling and starts qualifying. Founders trust agencies that say no. This sentence is doing more conversion work than any of the CTAs.

Honorable mentions: "fixed term. fixed scope. no auto-renew." appearing at three different surfaces is a brand drumbeat that gets stronger each time, not weaker. The "months standalone" counter pill on case-study cards is a brand-original metric that no competitor has — every glance at a card is a glance at the central thesis.

## Final verdict

**Indu fills the form.** Outcome: form-fill from the Verdant Ritual case study closing CTA route, not the homepage hero. Total session: 4:12. She comes back the next morning (Monday at 9:14 a.m.) on her laptop, re-reads the philosophy anchor, and forwards `/work/verdant-ritual` to her co-founder with the message "this is the model." Her co-founder reads it on the train and replies "talk to them today."

The rationale: the site does the founder-empathy work better than any agency site I've audited. The voice never breaks. The exit beat appears on every page, twice on most. The testimonial library is the strongest single asset — Sienna Holloway's quote is the kind of thing a founder reads at midnight and acts on. The friction points are all asset-shaped (real photography, real video, real hire), not voice-shaped. The brand is intact and the funnel works.

The one thing that would change the outcome from "fills the form" to "books a call directly": a phone number or a Calendly link. The site is asynchronous-first by design (founder-replies-not-chatbot), which is voice-correct. But for the 11:47 p.m. mobile founder with board pressure tomorrow, a one-click "talk to Aman this week" Calendly would close a deal class. **This is a positioning choice, not a fix.** The current async-first stance is more on-brand than a Calendly button would be. Logging as a future consideration, not a recommendation.

Beyond what's in `build-report.md`:

Move founder photography ahead of hero photography in the asset queue.

3. **Add `lastagency.com` to the contact form's email field as an autocomplete hint** for the "we send mail" mental model — minor. Not blocking. (Actually skip this. On reflection, autocomplete="email" is correct as-is. Disregard.)

5. **Consider a `/handoff` URL** that points to the most recent case-study handoff chapter as an anchor link. The closing CTA on services says "Get the playbook" — but the playbook is implied, not linked. A page or section called "the playbook" that shows three sample pages of a real handoff doc (redacted) would convert the founder who needs to see one concrete artifact before contacting. Brand-correct, conversion-correct. Worth Phase 5 thought.

6. **Add one "exit" beat to the footer.** Currently footer reads "your last agency. we mean that literally." That's strong. But the section labels (`site / office / elsewhere`) are quiet — one of them could carry a small voiced sub-line like "elsewhere — but we still answer email here." Optional polish.
