# Brand Voice Guide — Last Agency

> The master reference. Every agent reads this before writing a single word.
> Owner: Brand Guardian. Version: 1.0. Last updated: 2026-05-17.

## 1. Identity

**Name:** Last Agency
**Tagline:** *Your last agency. We mean that literally.*
**Central tension:** We are an agency built to make itself unnecessary. Every word, every visual, every interaction either reinforces that paradox or it's wrong. We earn the contract by promising to end it. The exit is the product.

**The 60-second elevator (how a founder describes us after one read of the homepage):**
"They're an agency that's trying to fire themselves. You hire them for 12 to 18 months — they build the function, run it, train your hire, hand over the playbook, and leave. Most agencies bill forever. These ones build to exit. The case studies are companies still running standalone two years later."

---

## 2. Voice pillars

1. **Cheeky, not snarky.** We punch up at industry conventions. Never down at clients.
   *Example:* "Most agencies bill forever. We bill for 14 months. You do the math."

2. **Founder-empathetic.** We talk like someone who's been in the trenches at 2 a.m. before a board meeting.
   *Example:* "You don't need another deck. You need the function to work by Q3."

3. **Anti-agency-theater.** No jargon. No synergy. No circling back. No pillars-of-our-philosophy.
   *Example:* "We don't do discovery workshops. We do the work."

4. **Confidently contrarian.** Every claim earns its keep with a number or a reason. Not edgy for edginess's sake.
   *Example:* "Retainers are a tax on indecision. We charge fixed-term."

5. **Specific over abstract.** We name the metric, the month, the playbook page.
   *Example:* "We trained Priya. She runs paid now. We're gone."

6. **Self-aware.** We know we're an agency. We're not a movement, a tribe, or a family.
   *Example:* "Yes, we're an agency. We're just allergic to the part where we never leave."

## 4. Do / Don't (with the *why*)

### ✅ Do

- **Short, declarative sentences.** *Why:* Founders skim. A sentence longer than 14 words is a sentence they didn't finish.
- **Specific numbers and timelines.** (12 months. 18 months. 5x ROAS. 1,200 sales.) *Why:* The only thing that separates us from every other agency claim is verifiable specificity.
- **Contractions** (we're, you'll, it's, don't). *Why:* Contractions read like a person talking. Without them you sound like a press release.
- **Speak to one founder, not "stakeholders."** *Why:* You're addressing the person whose name is on the cap table. "Stakeholders" is for an org chart.
- **Land the joke, then back it up.** *Why:* A joke without a reason underneath is a meme. We're not a meme.
- **Lowercase liberally in display copy.** *Why:* Lowercase is the visual cue for "this is a thought, not an announcement." It signals confidence — we don't need ALL CAPS to be heard.
- **Reference real founder pain** (CAC creep, board pressure, no in-house hire, retainer fatigue). *Why:* Specificity proves we've been there. Generic empathy proves we haven't.
- **Treat the exit as the product. Mention handoff/playbook at least once per page.** *Why:* Every page that doesn't reference the exit weakens the central tension by 20%.

### ❌ Don't

- **Agency clichés:** elevate, unlock, synergy, best-in-class, thought leadership, strategic partner, drive growth, passionate team. *Why:* These are the words a competitor uses. Using them puts us in the same row on a comparison sheet.
- **"We believe" statements.** *Why:* Belief is cheap. Replace with what we *do*. ("We believe in transparency" → "We send weekly numbers, even when they're bad.")
- **Exclamation marks** (one ironic use per page max). *Why:* Exclamation marks are the typographic equivalent of laughing at your own joke.
- **"Solutions" as a noun.** *Why:* "Solutions" means nothing. It's the word people use when they don't want to say what they actually sell.
- **"Journey" to describe a business process.** *Why:* A funnel is not a journey. A board meeting is not a journey. Save the word for travel.
- **Vague growth claims without numbers.** *Why:* "Significant growth" is what you write when the number is bad.
- **Generic CTAs** (Learn More, Get Started, Contact Us). *Why:* A generic CTA tells the visitor we ran out of opinions before we hit the button.
- **Stock metaphors:** unleash, ignite, transform, revolutionize. *Why:* These are verbs from a 2014 deck. They've been used into meaninglessness.

## 6. Banned phrases (the live list)

Seed list for `banned-phrases.json`. Brand Guardian appends every new offender caught in review.

**Agency clichés (hard-ban):**
elevate, unlock, synergy, best-in-class, thought leadership, strategic partner, drive growth, passionate team, holistic, end-to-end, full-service, world-class, cutting-edge, innovative, dynamic, results-driven, data-driven (when unsupported), boutique, premier, leading

**Corporate verbs:**
unleash, ignite, transform, revolutionize, empower, leverage, optimize (as standalone verb), maximize (without object), curate (overused), craft (overused), deliver (when not followed by a noun), enable

**Passive claims:**
"we believe", "we are passionate about", "we are committed to", "we strive to", "we pride ourselves on", "trusted by", "we love what we do"

**Vague growth language:**
"drive growth", "scale your business", "take to the next level", "exceed expectations", "exceptional results", "significant growth", "meaningful impact", "tangible outcomes"

**Dead metaphors:**
"journey" (when not literal travel), "ecosystem" (when not biology), "DNA" (when not genetics), "in our DNA", "ninja", "rockstar", "guru", "wizard", "magic"

**Phrases that flag a lazy sentence:**
"let's circle back", "at the end of the day", "moving the needle", "low-hanging fruit", "boil the ocean", "drink the kool-aid", "synergize", "ideate", "operationalize", "actionable insights", "value-add", "stakeholders" (use "founders" or the specific role), "solutions" (as a noun)

**Punctuation/formatting bans:**
Exclamation marks beyond one ironic per page. Em dashes are fine. Title Case Display Headlines (we prefer lowercase). All caps reserved for short anchors and CTAs only.

## 7. Visual rules

Restated from role file, with one specific commit at the end.

- **Type hierarchy is opinionated.** Display oversized (clamp 4rem–9rem). Body restrained (1rem–1.125rem). No mid-sized "feature heading" that doesn't earn its weight.
- **Lowercase allowed in display copy.** Required for the philosophy anchor and marquee. Sentence case for body. ALL CAPS reserved for: case-study anchor headings (e.g. "RESULTS, NOT PROPHECIES"), CTA labels, service tags optionally.
- **Color commits. One primary, one accent, one neutral. Stop.**
  - Background: warm off-black `#0E0E0C`
  - Text: off-white `#F4F1EA`
  - Accent: **amber/rust `#FF6B35`** (see commit below)
  - Muted: mid-grey `#8A8780`
- **No stock-photo energy.** Every image is purpose-built. Brand mocks, real campaign assets, real screenshots.
- **No skeuomorphism, no glassmorphism, no gradients-for-gradients-sake.** A single tonal gradient inside a CTA hover state is acceptable. Anything else, ask.
- **Animations have personality.** Custom cubic-bezier `(0.65, 0, 0.35, 1)` as default ease. No `ease-in-out` defaults.
- **Whitespace is generous.** Section padding minimum 8rem vertical on desktop. Crowding is a brand violation.
- **Micro-copy gets the same care as macro-copy.** Loading states, empty states, 404s, form errors — all voiced.

### Accent color commit: **amber/rust `#FF6B35`**

Why amber over chartreuse:
- **Chartreuse** signals tech-startup, hype-product, growth-hack. We are explicitly anti-hype. Chartreuse would put us in the same visual row as every Y Combinator landing page.
- **Amber/rust** signals craft, warmth, deliberate work, end-of-cycle (autumn — the season of harvest and exit). It's the color of a thing that has matured. It fits "we leave when the work is done" better than chartreuse fits "we exit."
- Amber pairs cleanly with the warm off-black and off-white — keeping the palette in a single temperature family. Chartreuse would fight the warm neutrals.
- It's also harder to find amber on a competitor's site. Differentiation by default.

**Locked.** Architect: use `#FF6B35` for the single accent. Hover states can shift to a slightly desaturated `#E85A2A`.

## 9. Brand anchors — committed decisions

The user asked us to commit without clarifying questions. These are locked.

### 9.1 Footer city: **Bangalore**

Why:
- "Bangalore" reads as the founder-tech capital of India to global readers and most domestic ones too. "Bengaluru" is the official name, but in the agency's voice — speaking to founders, not to bureaucracy — "Bangalore" is the spoken name. We optimize for the way our reader says it out loud.
- Single-word, recognized internationally, doesn't compete with the marquee for visual weight.
- If a regulator or a press kit needs "Bengaluru," that's a one-line override. The brand-facing version is **Bangalore.**

### 9.2 Portfolio card counter: **"months standalone"**

Why:
- TIW's "love counter" is decorative and personality-driven. Last Agency's equivalent must reinforce the central thesis: *the exit is the product.*
- "Standalone for 14 months and counting" tells the reader, on the card itself, that the client is still running without us. It's the single most differentiating data point we have.
- Format on card: a large number + the word "months" + the line "standalone." E.g. `18 / months / standalone.`
- Counter updates automatically based on `handoffDate` in `case-studies.json`.
- For brand-new exits (under 3 months): show "just shipped" instead. (Architect: handle the under-3-months case in the component.)

### 9.3 Voiced nav CTA: **"Talk to us"**

Why:
- "Hire us. Then unfollow us." is reserved for the hero. Putting it in the nav burns the line. The hero needs to be the first place the reader meets it.
- "Talk to us" is voiced (contracted, plural, direct) but quiet enough to live in the nav bar without competing with the page hero.
- "See the exit" or "Book the exit" were considered — both are too loud for nav placement. They earn their keep on /services and the closing block.
- Style: lowercase, no period, in the accent color `#FF6B35`. Underline on hover.

## 10. Escalation protocol

From the role file. Restated so other agents see it.

- **🔴 Rewrite required.** The originating agent has 2 attempts to fix. If the 3rd version still fails brand audit, Brand Guardian writes the correct version, logs the pattern to `voice-drift-log.md`, and notifies the orchestrator.
- **🟡 Needs revision.** Originating agent fixes per exact edits provided. No re-review needed unless the fix introduces a new violation.
- **✅ with a note.** FYI only. Note feeds into the next iteration of this guide.

**The two-attempt rule is real.** Don't waste rewrites on hedging. If you've used "leverage" twice, you've burned both attempts.
